    MarkMyAss shows VERIFIED CLEAN only when a supported signal was actually found before cleaning, its own re-inspection confirms it's gone, and every independent verifier that could run (ExifTool, and c2patool where applicable) agrees too. It is not a claim that the file contains no possible identifying signal whatsoever — only that the specific, supported metadata categories MarkMyAss targets are independently confirmed gone.     What is SynthID? Metadata vs the watermark inside the image

    An AI image can carry two very different kinds of “AI mark.” MarkMyAss is honest about which one it can actually touch.

    FILE-LEVEL

    Metadata & Content Credentials

    EXIF · XMP · PNG text · C2PA / Content Credentials — information stored alongside the picture.

    MarkMyAss can inspect & clean supported signals.

    CONTENT-LEVEL

    SynthID

    An invisible watermark Google embeds into the image's own pixels to indicate the content was generated or edited by AI — a separate technology from ordinary metadata.

    Removing metadata does not necessarily remove SynthID — and MarkMyAss does not claim to remove it.

    Google designed image SynthID to stay detectable after common changes such as resizing, recolouring, cropping, filtering and lossy compression, and says it remains detectable even when a file's metadata is stripped. Its published purpose is content provenance — answering “was this content generated or edited by AI?” Google describes SynthID as a content-provenance watermark, not as a personal tracking identifier; its documentation does not describe SynthID as containing your name, Google account, IP address or prompt history.

    A real check we ran

    We generated an image with Google's Gemini image model and ran it through MarkMyAss. It found Google's file-level provenance — XMP, PNG text and a C2PA manifest — and Clean removed those supported signals, so a second inspection showed 0 supported file-level signals. We then uploaded that same cleaned image to Gemini, which replied: “Based on the digital watermarking analysis, all or part of this image was created or edited using Google AI.”

    After MarkMyAss removed the supported file-level provenance and a second inspection showed 0 supported file-level signals, Gemini still identified the image as created or edited using Google AI based on digital watermarking analysis. This is consistent with a remaining content-level watermark such as SynthID, which is separate from metadata — though it does not prove SynthID alone was responsible. Cleaning metadata is not the same as removing SynthID — so we don't pretend it is.

    Why this exists — the EU AI Act (plain English)

    From 2 August 2026, EU AI Act Article 50 asks providers of generative AI to ensure synthetic audio, image, video or text outputs are “marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated.” It targets the outcome (detectability) and does not mandate SynthID — SynthID is Google's own technical approach, not an EU standard. Article 50(2) also carves out an exception where the AI performs “an assistive function for standard editing” or does not substantially alter the content or its meaning. This is background, not legal advice.

    AI EDITING

    Corrections & rewrites

    Proofreading, spelling, grammar, punctuation and light copy-editing — or larger rewrites.

    Using AI only to proofread, fix spelling/grammar/punctuation or lightly copy-edit is fundamentally different from having AI generate or substantially rewrite your document — a distinction the AI Act's Article 50(2) “standard editing” exception recognises too.

    Google's SynthID-Text is a statistical watermark created during token generation by slightly adjusting the model's token-selection probabilities. It is not ordinary metadata attached to your file — it lives in the pattern of the generated words themselves, and depends on how much of the text the model actually generated.
